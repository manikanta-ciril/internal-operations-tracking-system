Section 1 — What is CIRIL? 
CIRIL is a structured, collaborative commercial real estate brokerage network operating across 
India. It is not a single brokerage firm in the traditional sense. Instead, CIRIL functions as a 
franchise umbrella and transaction clearing-house that coordinates multiple independent member 
companies — each a separate real estate brokerage — under one networked platform.
 The word 'CRM' in CIRIL CRM stands for the custom-built software platform that manages the 
complete lifecycle of every real estate transaction that occurs within this network. The software is 
the operational backbone of the business.
 
1.1 The Core Business Idea 
Most commercial real estate transactions fail or are delayed because the broker who has the client 
(buyer or tenant) is geographically or informationally separate from the broker who has the right 
property. CIRIL solves this problem by creating a shared ecosystem:
 •�� A broker in Pune who has a client looking for a warehouse in Chennai is connected to a 
CIRIL partner broker in Chennai who has inventory.
 •�� The two brokers co-operate. The deal closes. The commission is split. CIRIL takes a royalty 
from both parties.
 •�� The entire process — from lead entry to final payment — is managed inside the CRM. 
1.2 Who are the Actors in the System? 
 
Actor / Role Responsibility Super Admin (roleId = 1) CIRIL's own master administrator. Has visibility over all leads, 
all member companies, all brokerage and royalty data network-wide. 
Zone Manager (roleId = 2) Oversees a specific geographic zone. Can see all leads within 
their zone. 
Member Company Admin Head of a CIRIL partner company. Creates users within their 
org, manages their own leads and network assignments. 
Broker / Agent (User) Day-to-day operator. Creates leads, assigns leads, negotiates

Research Team Feeds market intelligence data (rental trends, major deals, land 
prices) into the Research module.  
1.3 Geographic Hierarchy 
The system enforces a strict, multi-level geographic hierarchy that governs how leads are scoped and 
routed. Every lead is tagged to a location in this hierarchy:
 •�� Zone — The broadest level (e.g., South India, West India). •�� State — Contained within a Zone. •�� City — Contained within a State. This hierarchy means a Zonal Manager can filter the lead pipeline for his entire zone, while a 
member company is typically scoped to one or more cities.

Section 2 — What is a Lead? The Core Data Entity 
In CIRIL, a Lead is the most important entity in the entire system. It is the formal record of a client's 
real estate requirement. A lead is NOT a property listing — it is the CLIENT who needs the property.
 
2.1 Anatomy of a Lead 
A Lead in CIRIL is a composite entity assembled from FOUR separate database tables that are 
always joined together when querying:
  
Database Table Data It Contains leads The core record. Stores status flags, assignment metadata (user_id, 
assigned_to, assigned_orgid, assigned_user_id), negotiation document references (LOI file, Lease file), and all timestamps. 
client_details Who is the client? Stores client name, company name, contact info, 
document type (Aadhar, PAN, etc.), client type (individual or company). 
requirements_details What does the client need? Stores the vertical, transaction type, area range, 
budget, city, zone, and all vertical-specific technical parameters. 
financial_details How is the commission structured? Stores giver share %, receiver share %, 
giver and receiver names, their contact details, and brokerage status flags.

Section 3 — The Lead Giver & Lead Receiver: The 
Heart of CIRIL 
The most important conceptual distinction in the entire CIRIL model is the difference between the 
Lead Giver and the Lead Receiver. Every financial calculation in the system flows from this 
relationship.
 
3.1 The Lead Giver (LG) 
LEAD GIVER 
The broker or member company who originated the lead — i.e., the person 
who found the client and brought them into the CIRIL network. The Lead Giver owns the client relationship.  
In the database, the Lead Giver is identified by the leads.user_id field — the user who created the 
lead record. Their financial terms are stored in financial_details under financial_giver_share (their 
percentage of the total brokerage) and fields like financial_giver_name, financial_giver_phoneno.
 Scenario: Rajesh, a broker at CIRIL Mumbai, gets approached by TCS who wants 50,000 sq. ft. of 
office space in Bengaluru. Rajesh does not have Bengaluru inventory. He creates the lead in CIRIL 
CRM. Rajesh is now the Lead Giver.
 
3.2 The Lead Receiver (LR) 
LEAD RECEIVER 
The broker or member company who receives the assigned lead from the giver 
and takes responsibility for finding and closing the property transaction in their territory.  
In the database, the Lead Receiver is identified by the leads.assigned_to field (company) and 
leads.assigned_user_id (individual broker). Their financial terms are stored in financial_details 
under financial_receiver_share (their percentage of the total brokerage).
 Scenario: Priya, a broker at CIRIL Bengaluru, receives Rajesh's TCS lead. She searches her 
inventory, presents properties, manages site visits, and negotiates the lease. Priya is the Lead 
Receiver.
 
3.3 The Assignment Workflow in Code 
When a lead is assigned via the AssignleadController, the system updates three critical fields: •�� leads.assigned_to → set to the receiver's member company name/ID •�� leads.assigned_orgid → set to the receiver's organization ID (orgId) •�� leads.is_assigned → set to 1 (true) The receiver then sees this lead in their dashboard under 'Assign Lead'. They must explicitly click 
Accept Lead (acceptLead method) to confirm their participation, which updates leads.accepted = 1 
and records leads.accepted_datetime.
 •�� If the receiver rejects the lead, they call storeRejectLeadRemarks, which stores rejection 
remarks and resets the lead.

Section 4 — Revenue Engine: How CIRIL Makes 
Money 
CIRIL generates its revenue exclusively through Royalties — a fixed percentage cut taken from both 
the Lead Giver's share and the Lead Receiver's share of every successfully concluded deal's 
brokerage fee. The actual brokerage is split between LG and LR. CIRIL takes 7.5% from each party's 
share.
 The entire calculation is hard-coded in BrokerageController.php (getBrokerage method, line 202) 
and is fully automated — the system computes it the moment a deal reaches negotiation status.
 
4.1 Step 1 — Calculate the Base Deal Value (Transaction Value) 
The base value depends on whether the transaction is a Lease (monthly rent) or a Sale (outright 
purchase):
  LEASE 
Total Value = Rent Rate per sq. ft. per month × Area (sq. ft.) 
 SALE 
Total Value = Outright Rate per sq. ft. × Area (sq. ft.) 
4.2 Step 2 — Calculate Total Brokerage 
The brokerage due from the client is calculated differently for lease vs. sale:  
LEASE: Total Brokerage = Total Value × (Lease Days / 30) 
 
SALE:  Total Brokerage = 1% × Total Value 
 
The 'Lease Days' is the agreed number of days of rent that constitute the brokerage fee (a 
commercial real estate convention in India — typically 30-90 days of rent).
 
4.3 Step 3 — Split the Brokerage Between LG and LR 
The brokerage split is agreed upon upfront when the lead is created and stored in the 
FinancialDetail record. The fields financial_giver_share and financial_receiver_share contain the 
agreed percentage for each party. If either is set to 'others', the system reads 
financial_giver_share_others or financial_receiver_share_others for a custom percentage.
  
LG Share (Rs.) = (financial_giver_share / 100) × Total 
Brokerage  
LR Share (Rs.) = (financial_receiver_share / 100) × Total 
Brokerage

CIRIL Royalty = ROUND( (7.5/100 × LG Share) + (7.5/100 × LR

Section 5 — The Complete Deal Lifecycle (End to 
End) 
Every rupee of CIRIL's revenue follows this exact sequence. The CRM enforces this pipeline through 
status codes and controller logic.
 
Phase 1 — Lead Creation (LeadController@createLead) 
The broker (Lead Giver) opens the 'Create Lead' form. This is the most data-intensive step in the 
system. The form collects data that populates ALL FOUR lead tables simultaneously:
 •�� Client Details: Company name, person name, mobile, email, client type 
(individual/company), document type and file upload.
 •�� Requirements Details: Vertical selection (Office / Retail / Land / Warehouse / Investment / 
Hotels) → triggers a dynamic sub-form with vertical-specific fields.
 •�� Financial Details: The giver and receiver's expected shares, giver's brokerage terms — filled 
at creation based on negotiated terms.
 This design means a lead is never a partial record — all four tables must have data for a lead to be 
valid.

Once a vertical is selected, the requirements form dynamically loads unique fields for that property 
type. This is managed by VerticalType and VerticalSubType models and the CustomFieldsController.

Before or during negotiation, brokers can generate formal client proposals. The system supports two

 •�� First-Cut Proposal (type='F'): A quick preliminary proposal. For Land vertical, generates a

 •�� Detailed Proposal (type='D'): Full property analysis with image uploads for land sites. Same 
file format logic applies.
 Proposal files are saved to the /proposels/ directory and referenced in the proposels table with 
lead_id and type fields.
 
Phase 6 — Brokerage Calculation 
(BrokerageController@getBrokerage) 
When leads.lead_status reaches 4 (Negotiation/Deal Done) AND leads.negotiation_status_id 
reaches 7, the BrokerageController automatically calculates the full financial breakdown and writes 
it to the brokerage table. The brokerage record contains:
 •�� lead_id, clientType (lease/sale), area, rate (rentRate or outrightRate) •�� total (base deal value), totalBrokerage, LGshare (Rs.), LRshare (Rs.) •�� cirilRoyalty (Rs.) — CIRIL's revenue from this deal •�� status — empty until accepted, then 'accepted' •�� royality — 0 until royalty payment confirmed, then 1

(BrokerageController@brokerageStatus) 
The LR (the receiver who will collect and distribute the brokerage) formally accepts the calculated

Section 8 — Notifications & Real-Time Event System 
The CIRIL CRM includes a real-time notification system that keeps all parties informed as lead 
statuses change. This system uses Laravel's event broadcasting pattern.
 
8.1 The SendNotificationsEvent 
Every significant action in the system fires a SendNotificationsEvent. This event carries a payload 
with lead_id, url (the deep link to navigate to the relevant screen), and type (the category of 
notification). Key notification types:
  
Notification Type When It Fires first-cut-proposal Fired when a First-Cut Proposal is generated and available for 
download. 
royality-update Fired when royalty status is confirmed by the admin. lead-assigned Fired when a lead is assigned to a receiver company. lead-accepted Fired when the receiver formally accepts the lead. lead-rejected Fired when a receiver rejects an assigned lead. 
8.2 Notification Storage 
Notifications are persisted in the notifications table (mapped to the NotificationsModel). The 
NotificationsController provides three actions: mark_as_read (individual), clear_all (bulk), and 
view all (paginated list). This means even if a user is offline when an event fires, they see the 
notification on next login.

Section 10 — Guidelines, Brand Standards & 
Additional Modules 
10.1 Guidelines Module (GuideLinesController) 
CIRIL maintains operational guidelines accessible within the CRM: •�� Stationary Specification — Guidelines for how member companies must use CIRIL brand 
elements (logos, letterheads, business cards) on physical stationery.
 •�� Invoice Generation — Standard invoice template guidelines for billing clients for brokerage. •�� Data Mining Guide — Instructions on how to prospect and mine data for new client leads. •�� Property Listing Guide — Standards for how properties should be listed and described in

10.2 Individual Data Mining (IndividualMiningController) 
Brokers can log individual prospect contacts they are personally mining — people who have not yet 
committed to a formal lead. This acts as a pre-lead pipeline that brokers can convert into full leads 
when the prospect is ready. The model IndividualMining stores name, contact, property interest, 
and status.
 
10.3 Miscellaneous Approvals (MiscApprovalsController) 
Brokers can request approval to operate in additional 'Verticals' beyond their base assignment. For 
example, a broker primarily doing Office deals may request approval to also handle Warehouse 
leads. These requests are managed through the verticals approval workflow, where the Admin 
approves or denies the expansion of a broker's vertical scope.
 
10.4 Release Notes Module (ReleasenoteController) 
The CRM includes a formal Release Notes module where the CIRIL tech team publishes changelogs 
for new features and fixes. This is accessible to all users and serves as an in-app changelog for the 
platform itself.

11.3 What CIRIL Is NOT 
•�� It is NOT a property listing portal. There is no 'properties' table. The system manages client 
requirements and deal progress, not property inventory.
 •�� It is NOT a single-company CRM. It is explicitly multi-tenant, with each member company 
having its own isolated data view enforced by RBAC.
 •�� It is NOT an SPA (Single Page Application). Every page is a server-rendered Blade template. 
JavaScript is used for interactivity within pages but routing is server-side.
